Pre-Colonial Philippines

Tabon Caves

Palawan

Learn more

A cave complex on the southwest coast of Palawan that holds evidence of some of the earliest known human habitation in the Philippines.

Excavations here uncovered stone tools and human remains, including the skull cap and jaw fragments known as Tabon Man. These finds shed light on the people who first lived in the islands, long before any written record.

Pre-Colonial Philippines

Butuan Archaeological Sites

Agusan del Norte

Learn more

Home to the remains of ancient balangay boats, the wooden sailing vessels of early Filipino seafarers.

Butuan was once a busy trading settlement. The balangay, gold objects and other artifacts found here show the Philippines' early maritime technology, trade, and connections with neighboring Asian societies centuries before the Spanish arrived.

Pre-Colonial Philippines

Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras

Ifugao

Learn more

Terraced rice fields carved into the mountains of Ifugao, still farmed by indigenous communities today.

Built and maintained by hand over many generations, the terraces represent the agricultural knowledge, engineering, and traditions of the Ifugao people. They show that complex societies and cultural traditions existed long before Spanish colonization.

4

Pre-Colonial Philippines

Callao Cave

Peñablanca, Cagayan

Learn more

A large limestone cave system in Cagayan, with a small chapel inside its main chamber.

Fossils found in the Callao Cave system include a foot bone about 67,000 years old, and later finds led scientists to name a previously unknown human species, Homo luzonensis. The cave shows how early people lived in the islands tens of thousands of years ago.

Magellan and First Contact

Mactan Shrine

Cebu (1521)

Learn more

A memorial on Mactan Island that commemorates the Battle of Mactan.

In 1521, Lapu-Lapu and his warriors defeated the forces of the explorer Ferdinand Magellan, who was killed in the fighting. The shrine represents early Filipino resistance against foreign intervention.

📍 Google Maps Book your trip
6

Magellan and First Contact

Magellan's Cross and Basilica Minore del Santo Niño

Cebu (1521 onward)

Learn more

A cross and a church in the heart of Cebu City, linked to the first arrival of Christianity in the islands.

Magellan's expedition planted a cross here in 1521 and the first baptisms followed. The neighboring Basilica Minore del Santo Niño keeps the image of the Santo Niño, so the site marks the beginning of a major religious and cultural transformation in the archipelago.

Spanish Colonial Period

Intramuros and Fort Santiago

Manila (1571 onward)

Learn more

The walled city of Manila, which became the center of Spanish colonial government.

Fort Santiago, at the mouth of the Pasig River, served as a military headquarters and a prison during different periods of Philippine history. Together they represent Spanish political, military, and administrative control over the islands.

8

Spanish Colonial Period

Historic City of Vigan

Ilocos Sur

Learn more

A cobblestone town of old mansions and narrow streets, and one of the best-preserved Spanish colonial towns in Asia.

Vigan shows how Filipino, Chinese, and European influences blended to shape colonial Philippine architecture and culture. Its Calle Crisologo is lined with ancestral houses, and the town was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999.

Spanish Colonial Period

San Agustin Church

Manila (1571 onward)

Learn more

The oldest stone church in the Philippines, inside Intramuros.

It represents the influence of Spanish Catholicism and religious institutions. Its survival through wars and destruction makes it an important architectural and historical landmark.

Spanish Colonial Period

Fort San Pedro

Cebu City (1565 onward)

Learn more

A small triangular stone fort on the Cebu waterfront, built by the Spanish.

Construction began in 1565 as part of Miguel López de Legazpi's settlement. Over the centuries it served as a military base and a prison, and it shows how the Spanish defended their early footholds in the islands.

Spanish Colonial Period

Paoay Church

Ilocos Norte

Learn more

A church famous for the huge stone buttresses along its sides.

Completed in 1710, it is one of the Baroque Churches of the Philippines listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its design shows how builders adapted European church architecture to a land of earthquakes.

Spanish Colonial Period

Miagao Church

Iloilo

Learn more

A golden-yellow fortress church on a hill above the town of Miagao.

Built between 1786 and 1797, its thick walls and tall towers helped protect the town from raiders. It is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a good example of Spanish colonial church-building in the Visayas.

Revolution and the First Republic

Rizal Park (Luneta)

Manila (1896)

Learn more

The great public park of Manila, and the place where José Rizal was executed.

Rizal was executed here on December 30, 1896. His death became a major inspiration for Filipino nationalism and the revolutionary movement. Today the park holds the Rizal Monument and the Kilometer Zero marker, and it is a popular place to walk.

Revolution and the First Republic

Pinaglabanan Memorial Shrine

San Juan (1896)

Learn more

A shrine in San Juan that commemorates the Battle of Pinaglabanan.

The battle, fought on August 30, 1896, was one of the earliest major clashes of the Philippine Revolution, and the shrine represents the beginning of the armed struggle against Spanish rule.

Revolution and the First Republic

Biak-na-Bato National Park

San Miguel, Bulacan (1897)

Learn more

A mountain and cave area in Bulacan that served as a hideout for revolutionaries.

Emilio Aguinaldo and his forces set up a republic here and signed the Pact of Biak-na-Bato with Spain in December 1897, which ended the fighting for a time and sent Aguinaldo to exile in Hong Kong.

Revolution and the First Republic

Barasoain Church

Bulacan (1898–1899)

Learn more

The church in Malolos that hosted the Malolos Congress.

The congress is associated with the creation of the Malolos Constitution and the First Philippine Republic. The site represents the Filipinos' effort to establish an independent government.

Revolution and the First Republic

Aguinaldo Shrine

Cavite (June 12, 1898)

Learn more

The house in Kawit where Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence from Spain.

The proclamation on June 12, 1898 is one of the most important moments in the country's struggle for nationhood. Aguinaldo's house, now a museum, is also key to understanding the early Philippine Republic and the transition from Spanish to American rule.

Revolution and the First Republic

Malolos Historic Town

Bulacan (1899)

Learn more

The capital of the First Philippine Republic.

The town represents the establishment of an organized Filipino government before American colonial rule was fully established. The Malolos Congress met here, and Emilio Aguinaldo was inaugurated as the first president of the republic in January 1899.

World War II

Bataan Death March Memorial and Capas National Shrine

Bataan and Tarlac (1942)

Learn more

Memorials to the Filipino and American soldiers who suffered during the Bataan Death March.

The sites represent the sacrifices and suffering experienced during the Japanese invasion of the Philippines. In April 1942, thousands of prisoners of war were forced to march for days under harsh conditions toward prison camps in Tarlac.

World War II

Corregidor Island

Manila Bay (1942–1945)

Learn more

A rocky island fortress at the entrance to Manila Bay.

Corregidor served as a major defensive position during the Japanese invasion and became one of the last major American-Filipino strongholds before the fall of the Philippines in 1942.

World War II

Manila American Cemetery and Memorial

Taguig, Metro Manila

Learn more

A vast memorial of white crosses honoring Americans and Allies who died in the Pacific war.

It holds the graves of about 17,000 military dead, the largest group of American World War II graves outside the United States, and walls that name thousands more who were never found.

World War II

MacArthur Landing Memorial National Park

Leyte (October 20, 1944)

Learn more

A memorial park on the Leyte shore where General Douglas MacArthur landed.

The landing marks the beginning of the major Allied campaign to liberate the Philippines from Japanese occupation. MacArthur waded ashore with President Sergio Osmeña, keeping his 1942 promise to return.

Post-War Republic

EDSA Shrine and People Power Monument

Quezon City (1986)

Learn more

A church and a bronze monument that remember the February 1986 People Power Revolution on EDSA.

The Shrine of Mary, Queen of Peace was built in 1989 to commemorate the peaceful uprising that ended the Marcos government. A little farther along EDSA, the People Power Monument shows 37 figures from many parts of Philippine society.

25

Post-War Republic

Bantayog ng mga Bayani

Quezon City (1972–1986)

Learn more

A memorial and museum for the people who stood against martial law.

Its black granite Wall of Remembrance carries the names of martyrs and heroes of the struggle against martial law. The monument and the wall were unveiled in 1992, and new names are added to the wall each year.
