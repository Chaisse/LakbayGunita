Before 1521

Pre-Colonial Philippines

Long before any European ship appeared on the horizon, people were living, trading, farming, and sailing in the islands. These four places show how far back Philippine history goes and how advanced the first societies were.

About this period

The earliest signs of people in the Philippines are tens of thousands of years old. Over time, communities learned to farm, to build boats, to work gold, and to trade with their neighbors in Asia. Their stories survive in caves, river mud, and mountain terraces.

Palawan

1Tabon Caves

A cave complex on the southwest coast of Palawan that holds evidence of some of the earliest known human habitation in the Philippines.

About the place

The Tabon Cave complex sits on Lipuun Point on the southwest coast of Palawan. It is a network of limestone caves, only some of which have been excavated. Archaeologists from the National Museum began digging here in the 1960s and recovered stone flake tools, charcoal from ancient hearths, and burial remains.

Its part in our history

Tabon is one of the main places that tell us people were living in the Philippines tens of thousands of years ago. The bones known as Tabon Man, a skull cap and jaw fragments, are among the oldest human remains found in the country. Nearby Manunggul Cave gave up the Manunggul Jar, a burial jar with two figures in a boat that shows early beliefs about the journey of the soul. Together they show that the first Filipinos were skilled toolmakers with a rich spiritual life.

Agusan del Norte

2Butuan Archaeological Sites

Home to the remains of ancient balangay boats, the wooden sailing vessels of early Filipino seafarers.

About the place

Butuan lies on the Agusan River in northeastern Mindanao. Digging in and around the city since the 1970s has uncovered wooden boats, burial coffins, ceramics, and gold ornaments buried in the old riverbanks.

Its part in our history

The boats found here are balangay, the plank-built sailing vessels that gave the word barangay to Filipino communities. Radiocarbon tests place some of them in the first millennium AD, which proves that Filipinos were building ocean-going ships long before European contact. Chinese records from around the year 1000 mention Butuan sending trade missions, and its goldwork and imported porcelain show a wealthy port in touch with the wider Asian world. Butuan reminds us that early Philippine society was connected and prosperous, not isolated.

Ifugao

3Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras

Terraced rice fields carved into the mountains of Ifugao, still farmed by indigenous communities today.

About the place

The rice terraces of the Ifugao province in the Cordillera mountains climb the slopes in huge stone-walled steps. UNESCO recognized five clusters of them, including those at Banaue, Batad, Bangaan, Mayoyao, and Hungduan, as a World Heritage landscape in 1995.

Its part in our history

The terraces are often said to be about 2,000 years old, although some researchers suggest they are younger. Either way, they were built and repaired by hand, fed by irrigation channels from the forests above, and kept alive by Ifugao rituals, planting calendars, and the chanted epics called hudhud. The Ifugao and other Cordillera peoples were never fully brought under Spanish rule, so the terraces are a living link to Filipino traditions that carried on through the colonial centuries and still do today.

Peñablanca, Cagayan

4Callao Cave

A large limestone cave system in Cagayan, with a small chapel inside its main chamber.

About the place

Callao Cave is part of a large limestone cave system near Peñablanca in Cagayan. Its main chamber has a natural skylight and holds a small chapel with an altar, which is still used for Mass.

Its part in our history

In 2007 archaeologists found a small foot bone in the Callao Cave system that is about 67,000 years old, making it one of the oldest known human fossils in the Philippines. Further finds in the cave led scientists in 2019 to describe a new species of early human, Homo luzonensis, a small-bodied relative that lived on Luzon. The discovery changed what researchers thought about how early people reached and lived on the islands, and it placed the Philippines in the story of human evolution in Asia.
